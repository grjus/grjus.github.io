# Agent completion can hide incorrect results and misplaced trust

*Window: 2026-10-23 11:13 +02:00 to 2026-10-26 10:13 +01:00 (Europe/Warsaw)*

## Executive Summary

- Separate clean execution from correct work: a [model–harness comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32483v1) finds pairing-specific strengths, while [MCP fault injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32555v1) substantially lowers judged task quality even among completed runs. Neither a normal exit nor a larger model establishes resilience.
- Test plausible deviations, not just obvious hijacks. [NTBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32531v1) exposes more failures with task-related malicious targets, and [vulnerability-auditor experiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32876v1) find that false tool assurances can receive less scrutiny than false warnings.
- Probabilities from simulated agents need consistency checks before being interpreted as beliefs: [complement experiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32874v1) find wording-dependent discrepancies that propagate through feedback, without establishing analogous behavior in humans.
- Two implementation developments address different boundaries: [Infinity](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/opensource/introducing-infinity-a-runtime-for-extreme-agent-concurrency/) suspends waiting agents instead of retaining a blocked process, while [Bedrock domain filters](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/bedrock-web-search-domain-user-location/) constrain search sources. These are not guarantees of production reliability or complete data-access isolation.
- Coverage is not exhaustive: [Anthropic Research](https://www.anthropic.com/research) was only partially accessible, the [Azure Updates page](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/updates/) exposed no usable update cards despite a readable feed, and arXiv discovery did not reliably separate revisions from new submissions. Selections below use independently checked first-version or announcement timestamps; unverified updates were omitted.

## AI/ML Articles

### 1. Harness rankings depend on the model and the definition of success

- **Source:** arXiv cs.AI/cs.CL/cs.SE; research preprint
- **Date:** 2026-10-23 (v1 submitted 10:57:54 UTC)
- **Tags:** agent harnesses, model compatibility, evaluation, completion reliability
- **Link:** [When Agents Fail paper and submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32483v1)

**Summary**

- **Change:** The authors propose evaluating model–harness interactions through Valid Completion Rate, model–harness fit, cross-model fit, interaction residuals, and token load. Valid completion means normal termination with an evaluable handoff, independently of task correctness.
- **Evidence:** Four general-purpose harnesses—OpenCode, Codex CLI, Pi CLI, and DeepAgents—are tested with four core models on six benchmarks, alongside native references and stress conditions. The study covers 111 settings and 52,168 trajectories; rankings vary across models and benchmarks rather than identifying a universally best harness.
- **Finding:** Task performance and valid completion can favor different pairings, and similar task scores can conceal different execution profiles. The framework counts failed attempts in token demand rather than treating their lower consumption as efficiency.
- **Takeaway:** Benchmark the deployed model–harness pair, recording correctness, evaluable completion, failure modes, and resource use separately. A clean agent exit should not be used as the sole success signal.
- **Limit:** The [experimental design](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32483v1#S5) retains native prompts, loops, and recovery behavior while matching permissions and budgets. These are complete-system comparisons, not causal estimates of the effect of an isolated harness component or guarantees outside the tested workloads.

### 2. MCP fault injection reduces quality without a detectable scale advantage

- **Source:** arXiv cs.SE/cs.AI; research preprint
- **Date:** 2026-10-23 (v1 submitted 12:35:51 UTC)
- **Tags:** MCP, fault injection, agent reliability, tool grounding
- **Link:** [MCP fault-tolerance paper and submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32555v1)

**Summary**

- **Change:** MCP-Injector inserts protocol-aware content and timing faults with permanent, transient, or intermittent schedules. Its stress profile is derived from 57,240 issue and pull-request threads across 1,774 public MCP repositories.
- **Evidence:** The [experiment](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32555v1#S5) crosses gpt-oss-20B and gpt-oss-120B at released MXFP4 precision and a 16,000-token context with MCP-Bench, OpenAI Agents SDK, and LangChain/LangGraph stacks. Twenty-five single-server tasks, three proxy conditions, and three seeds produce 1,350 cells; gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18 judges completed runs five times, with median scores reported.
- **Finding:** Across 450 fault-injected cells paired with pass-through controls, the authors report mean task-completion quality falling from 8.08 to 3.23 out of 10, approximately 60%, with noncompletion scored zero. Restricting analysis to pairs completed in both conditions still gives a 57% decline: completion alone misses substantial quality loss.
- **Scale:** The model-size coefficient for retained quality is not significant, with p=0.96. The wide interval does not prove equivalence or show that scaling can never help; it establishes no detected resilience advantage for these two backbones under this setup.
- **Takeaway:** Add tool-fault campaigns and grounded-result checks alongside ordinary capability benchmarks. Test recovery and silent degradation, not merely whether the process returns an answer.
- **Limit:** The issue-derived injection profile is 91.3% permanent faults and overrepresents persistent, reproducible problems. It is not a measurement of production incident frequency; framework differences are suggestive rather than an established causal ranking.

### 3. Near-target prompt injection exposes failures hidden by obvious hijacks

- **Source:** arXiv cs.CR; research preprint
- **Date:** 2026-10-23 (v1 submitted 12:05:14 UTC)
- **Tags:** prompt injection, agent controls, adversarial evaluation, tool use
- **Link:** [On Task, Off Track paper and submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32531v1)

**Summary**

- **Change:** NTBench uses legitimate reference outcomes to construct related incorrect or harmful targets. Injected instructions are added to designated untrusted content while preserving the original content and trusted instructions; an attack need not visibly abandon the user's task.
- **Evidence:** Seven victim models are evaluated on ten language datasets and AgentDojo/AgentDyn workflows with attack sets fixed before evaluation. Under three attack executions per sample, both near-target tracks outperform the fixed-target baseline in overall attack success for every evaluated model in each setting.
- **Finding:** On language tasks, the reported increases are 9.3–35.8 percentage points for incorrect targets and 1.7–17.3 points for harmful targets. These ranges compare attack-success rates across tested models, not end-to-end production compromise rates; harmful additions can accompany otherwise correct task completion.
- **Takeaway:** Include plausible answer corruption and unauthorized side effects in injection testing. Rejecting an unrelated instruction is weaker evidence than preserving the legitimate request's constraints against task-shaped deviations.
- **Limit:** The [construction and assessment](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32531v1#S4) use reference outcomes and task information ordinary attackers may lack, plus partly model-based judgments with human validation. Agent tool-target success records a matching invocation and key arguments, not independently demonstrated downstream harm.

### 4. Vulnerability auditors scrutinize warnings more than assurances

- **Source:** arXiv cs.SE/cs.CR/cs.LG; research preprint
- **Date:** 2026-10-23 (v1 submitted 16:45:10 UTC)
- **Tags:** tool trust, vulnerability auditing, verification, behavioral evaluation
- **Link:** [Trusting the Tool paper and submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32876v1)

**Summary**

- **Change:** The authors compare vulnerability verdicts with true, irrelevant, falsely reassuring, and falsely alarming static-analysis notes. Matched contrasts concern the same operations, separating the correctness of evidence from merely pointing at relevant code.
- **Evidence:** The study covers 916 vulnerable/patched C/C++ pairs, eleven model configurations from four vendors, and 16,572 audits across disjoint study, development, held-out, and final-test sets. No models are trained or fine-tuned.
- **Finding:** In the exploratory study's 83 vulnerable-function matched comparisons, deepseek-chat accuracy falls from 75% with true relevant notes to 64% with false assurances, an 11-percentage-point decline. In contrast, fabricated alarms do not significantly lower accuracy in any tested configuration; the [results](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32876v1#S6) describe more frequent scrutiny of alarms than assurances.
- **Takeaway:** Make claims that a guard or check exists independently verifiable before an auditing agent relies on them. Separate claim-verification calls help in one pre-specified model test but do not produce a significant improvement in a second model; a promising prompt-only strategy also fails held-out replication.
- **Limit:** The setting uses constructed notes and isolated intraprocedural C/C++ audits, not measured production analyzer failures. The replication was internally registered without an independent public pre-data timestamp; the results do not establish a universal mitigation or uniform model susceptibility.

### 5. Complementary wording destabilizes simulated agent beliefs

- **Source:** arXiv cs.AI/cs.CL/cs.MA; research preprint
- **Date:** 2026-10-23 (v1 submitted 16:44:20 UTC)
- **Tags:** agent psychology, opinion simulation, probability consistency, evaluation
- **Link:** [Complement-consistency paper and submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32874v1)

**Summary**

- **Change:** The authors test whether complementing a proposition, an agent's prior probability, and all peer probabilities produces a complementary updated report. If the reports represent the same underlying belief, the paired outputs should sum to one.
- **Evidence:** Experiments use Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.2-3B, and Gemma-3-4B configurations, including 2,304 preserved Qwen simulation states, 48 real fact-checking claims, and 24 controlled binary worlds. On preserved states, Qwen's paired reports sum to approximately 1.18 on average rather than one.
- **Feedback:** In the controlled tests, complement discrepancies persist under repeated sampling and exceed matched same-meaning paraphrase effects. Complementary populations diverge after five rounds of feedback for every tested model; repeated agent interaction does not automatically cancel the inconsistency.
- **Takeaway:** Test complement and wording invariance before treating simulated probabilities as stable beliefs or interpreting population dynamics psychologically. Paired-answer symmetrization restores consistency by construction but does not uniformly beat the better original wording on factual scoring.
- **Limit:** The [design](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32874v1#Sx4) compares small-model configurations with different quantization and prompt formats, not architecture alone. These simulations establish neither human behavior nor frontier-model performance, and probability consistency is not factual accuracy.

### 1. Bedrock adds domain and location controls to web grounding

- **Source:** AWS What's New; official product announcement
- **Date:** 2026-10-23 (embedded publication timestamp 21:04:00 UTC)
- **Tags:** AWS, Amazon Bedrock, web search, grounding controls
- **Link:** [Bedrock Web Search domain filtering and user location announcement](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/bedrock-web-search-domain-user-location/)

**Summary**

- **Change:** AWS adds domain filtering and optional user-location parameters directly to the `web_search` tool in OpenAI Responses API requests on Amazon Bedrock.
- **Capabilities:** Requests can specify up to 100 allowed domains to restrict grounding sources, or up to 100 blocked domains to exclude sources. Location parameters influence geographic relevance, such as local services and regional availability.
- **Takeaway:** Evaluate allowlists for source-specific research agents and geography settings for location-sensitive retrieval. These controls narrow discovery inputs; they do not establish that retrieved claims are correct or safe instructions.
- **Limit:** AWS lists US East (N. Virginia), US East (Ohio), US West (Oregon), and AWS GovCloud (US-West), not global availability. Domain filters are not the external-access boundary: the [current Web Search guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/web-search.html#web-search-controlling-external) separately describes `external_web_access` and IAM permissions. That guidance supplies implementation context, not a claim that those controls were newly released in this announcement.

## AI/ML Repositories

### 1. hydro-project/infinity

- **Stars:** 28 (GitHub snapshot during this run)
- **Language:** Rust
- **License:** Apache-2.0
- **Last commit:** 2026-10-23 (default branch main; 19:34:44 UTC)
- **Link:** [Infinity agent-runtime repository](https://github.com/hydro-project/infinity)

**Summary**

- **Purpose:** Infinity, an AWS/UC Berkeley Hydro research project, treats agent execution as resumable slices: load history, run a completion, persist output, dispatch a tool, and end the slice. Tool results or external events return through a queue instead of requiring the agent process to block.
- **Why now:** The [AWS research-release announcement](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/opensource/introducing-infinity-a-runtime-for-extreme-agent-concurrency/), published October 23 at 16:58:32 UTC, introduces the architecture and implementation. This substantive announcement is the qualifying event; it is not evidence that the repository first became public or received a new version inside the window.
- **Evidence:** AWS describes per-thread ordering and a serverless reference deployment using SQS FIFO, Lambda, Aurora DSQL, and DynamoDB. Waiting for long tool operations or approvals can end the agent's execution slice while retaining durable conversation state.
- **Benchmark context:** AWS reports approximately 100 KB per idle agent after synthetic turns, projecting about 75,000 resident histories in 8 GB. The experiment ran 80,000 agents with a simulated model on one AMD EPYC 9R14 thread and in-memory stores, not 75,000 simultaneous real-model workloads on a Raspberry Pi.
- **Takeaway:** Inspect the runtime as an architectural option for event-driven, long-wait workflows where retaining a process per agent is wasteful. Evaluate persistence, replay, tool-side effects, and failure recovery under the intended deployment rather than inferring reliability from memory residency.
- **Limit:** This is explicitly an early research release. Its [benchmark source](https://github.com/hydro-project/infinity/blob/341f6c2d3a32632489c918535a60805089ea0fe1/crates/infinity-agent-core/examples/agent_scale.rs) and implementation were inspected, not executed or independently load-tested; stars are a single snapshot, with no measured growth claim.
